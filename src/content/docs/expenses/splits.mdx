Under **Split between** on an expense, pick who it was for by tapping their names, then pick how to split it: **Equal**, **Exact**, **Percent**, **Shares**, **Adjust** or **By item**. On a phone the row of choices scrolls sideways. Whatever you choose, the shares always add up to the total, to the cent.

## Equal

Everyone pays the same. This is the default, and the right answer most of the time.

> A $60 grocery run split between Ava, Ben and Cam: $20.00 each.

Tap a name to take someone out of the split, and tap again to bring them back. The line under the list shows what each person pays, for example **$20.00 each**.

## Exact

Type in what each person owes. Use it when you already know the numbers, like a shared delivery order where everyone picked their own meal.

> A $47 takeout order: Ava $18, Ben $16.50, Cam $12.50.

As you type, Potluck tells you how far off you are: **$5.00 left to assign**, **$2.00 too much**, or **Everything adds up**. Tap **Even it out** to start again from equal amounts. You cannot save until it adds up.

## Percent

Slide until it feels fair. The percentages must add up to 100%.

<Screenshot
  name="expense-new-percent"
  alt="The Percent split. Six people are ticked: Ike, Jo, Kai, Lena, You and Noor. A colored bar is divided into six segments of 17 percent each, with a handle between each pair of neighbors. Below the bar, Ike's row shows 16.67 percent and $20.00."
/>

Each person gets a colored segment on one bar. Drag the handle between two people to move share from one to the other. Only those two neighbors change, so the total stays at exactly 100%. You can also type a percentage in each person's row (two decimals are fine, like 16.67), or tap **Even it out** to reset.

> A $1,000 cabin weekend where Ava and Ben took the big room: Ava 30%, Ben 30%, Cam 20%, Dev 20%. That is $300, $300, $200 and $200.

:::tip[Keyboard]
Tab to a handle and use the arrow keys to move it one percent at a time. Hold Shift for 5%, use Page Up and Page Down for 10%, and Home or End to push it all the way to one side.
:::

## Shares

Give each person a number of shares and the cost is divided in proportion. Couples count as 2, the kid counts as half.

> A $120 dinner. Ravi and Sam came as a couple (2 shares), Ike and Jo came alone (1 share each). That is 4 shares of $30: Ravi and Sam pay $60 together, Ike $30, Jo $30.

Use the minus and plus buttons next to each name, or type a number. Half shares and other fractions are fine.

## Adjustment

Everyone pays the same, except for a few dollars here and there. Tap **Adjust**, then add or take off an amount for anyone who should pay more or less. Potluck takes the adjustments off the top and splits the rest equally.

> A $96 dinner for Ava, Ben and Cam. Ben ordered a $15 bottle of wine just for himself, so give him +$15. That leaves $81 to split equally: Ava $27, Ben $27 + $15 = $42, Cam $27.

To take something off, tap the minus button next to the person's amount. Say Cam skipped dessert and should pay $6 less. With Ben at +$15 and Cam at -$6, the equal part is $96 - $15 + $6 = $87, or $29 each. Ava pays $29, Ben $44 and Cam $23.

The line under the list shows the equal part, for example **$87.00 split equally, then the adjustments**. Leave a person's adjustment empty and they simply pay the equal part.

Two things are not allowed:

- Adjustments that add up to more than the total: **The adjustments add up to more than the total.**
- A minus that takes someone below zero: **A minus adjustment takes someone below zero.**

## By item

For restaurant bills where people ordered different things. Assign each item to the people who had it, and tax and tip follow along.

<Screenshot
  name="expense-new-by-item"
  alt="The By item split. One item, Pasta and wine, costs 148 and is shared by You, Ike, Jo, Kai, Lena and Noor. The totals box shows Subtotal $148.00, Tax 19.24, Tip 26.64 with the 18% chip selected among 15%, 18% and 20%, and Total $193.88. Below, Ike, Jo and You owe $32.31 each, while Kai and Lena owe $32.32."
/>

1. Type the item's name and **Price**, then tap the names of **Who had it?** An item shared by three is split equally between those three.
2. Tap **Add item** for the next one.
3. Enter the **Tax** and the **Tip**. The **15%**, **18%** and **20%** chips fill in the tip as a percentage of the subtotal. Tap a chip again to clear it, or type your own tip.
4. Got a coupon or a happy hour deal? Enter it under **Discount**.

The **Total** is the subtotal plus tax and tip, minus the discount. It comes from the items, so you do not type an amount at the top.

Tax, tip and discount are split in proportion to what each person ordered. If your food was a third of the subtotal, you pay a third of the tax and tip, and you get a third of the discount.

> Pizza $30 (Ava and Ben), salad $12 (Ben), drinks $18 (Ava and Cam). Subtotal $60: Ava had $24, Ben $27, Cam $9. Add $6 tax and a $12 tip, $18 in all.
>
> Ava had 40% of the food, so she pays 40% of the $18: $7.20, for $31.20. Ben had 45%: $8.10 more, $35.10. Cam had 15%: $2.70 more, $11.70. Together that is the $78 total.

:::note[Good to know]
Potluck keeps the bill as you entered it: every item, who had it, and the tax, tip and discount. The expense page lists them under **Items**. When you edit the expense, the items open again, ready to change. By-item expenses saved before Potluck kept items open as **Exact** amounts instead, with a note saying so.
:::

## Default split

If your group splits most things the same way, save it as the group's default split. Every new expense in that group then starts with it, and you only change it for the odd one out.

> Ravi and Sam share a flat with Ike. Ravi and Sam have the big room, so rent and bills are split by shares: Ravi 2, Sam 2, Ike 1. Save that once, and every new expense in the group opens that way.

A default split can be **Equal** (between everyone or only some people), **Percent** or **Shares**. **Exact**, **Adjust** and **By item** depend on the amount, so they cannot be a default.

There are two places to set it.

**From an expense**
: Set up the split the way you want on **New expense**. Tick **Use this split for new expenses in this group** under the split, then save the expense.

**From group settings**
: Open the group's **Settings** tab and find **Default split**. Tap **Change**, pick the people and the split, and tap **Save default split**. See [Group settings](/groups/settings/#default-split).

When a new expense opens with the default, a note under the split says **This is the group default for new expenses.** Tap **Back to equal** there, or in settings, to go back to an equal split between everyone.

Good to know about the default split:

- It applies to that group only. Your other groups keep their own default, or none.
- Anyone in the group can change it, not only owners and admins.
- It only shapes where new expenses start. It never changes an expense that is already saved, and it never moves a balance.
- People who have left the group are dropped from it. New members are included automatically when the default is **Equal** between everyone.

## How rounding works

Money only comes in whole cents, and splits rarely divide evenly. Potluck handles it the same way every time:

1. It works out everyone's exact share, fractions of a cent included.
2. It rounds every share down to the cent.
3. That leaves a few cents unassigned. They go out one cent at a time, starting with the people whose shares were rounded down the most.
4. When there is a tie, a fixed order inside Potluck breaks it, so the same expense always rounds the same way.

Nobody ever pays more than one cent above their exact share, and the shares always add up to the total.

The by-item screenshot above shows it. $193.88 split six ways is $32.3133 each. Rounded down, six times $32.31 is $193.86, which leaves 2 cents. Those 2 cents go to two people, so Kai and Lena pay $32.32 and everyone else pays $32.31.

By-item bills are rounded once over the whole bill, not item by item, so small roundings do not pile up.
