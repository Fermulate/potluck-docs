Potluck is free software with no ads and no tracking code. It stores what it needs to split money and plan things, and shows it only to your friends and the people you share a group with.

## What is stored, and who sees it

| What | Who can see it |
| --- | --- |
| Your email and password | Nobody in the app. Your password is stored scrambled (hashed), never as text. |
| Your two-step sign in key and recovery codes | Nobody in the app. Recovery codes are stored scrambled too. |
| Your name, photo, time zone and default currency | You, your [friends](/friends/), and people you share a group with. People still in a group you left keep seeing them. Anyone who opens your [friend link](/friends/#your-friend-link) sees your first name and photo. |
| Your [payment methods](/account/#payment-methods) | You, and people you share a group or [one-on-one tab](/friends/#split-something-with-one-friend) with, unless either of you blocked the other. A friend you share neither with does not see them. |
| Groups, members and roles | The members of that group |
| A group's [cover photo](/groups/settings/#cover-photo) | The members of that group, and anyone who opens one of its invite links. The image itself is a public link, like the group's name on the invite page. |
| A group's [notes](/groups/#group-notes) | The members of that group |
| Expenses, splits, payments and comments | The members of that group |
| Receipts | The members of that group. They are private files, not public links. |
| Plans, availability and RSVPs | The members of that group |
| Invite links | Owners and admins, and whoever made the link |
| The name of someone [not on Potluck yet](/groups/#people-not-on-potluck-yet) | The members of that group |
| The email saved for someone not on Potluck yet | Nobody in the app. Members see part of it, like s\*\*\*@example.com. |
| Who you blocked | Only you. Nobody is ever told they were blocked. |
| Reports you send | The Potluck team. The person you reported is not told. |
| Notifications and your push and email notification settings | Only you |

Someone who leaves a group, or is removed, loses access to it right away.

## What your backup contains

The [backup](/account/#download-a-backup) on **Me** holds only what you can already see in the app. The full list is under [Download a backup](/account/#download-a-backup). For your privacy and other people's:

- It has your own payment methods, never anyone else's.
- It names the other people in your groups, but has no one else's email, not even the one saved for someone not on Potluck yet.
- It leaves out groups that were [deleted](/groups/settings/#delete-the-group), even while they can still be restored.

It does **not** hold your password, your two-step sign in key or recovery codes, receipt photos, your calendar feed link, your notifications or who you blocked.

The file is made on Potluck's servers and saved straight to your device. Nothing is sent anywhere else. Once it is saved, it is yours to keep safe: it has your groups' history and your friends' names in it.

## People not on Potluck yet

When you [add someone by name](/groups/#people-not-on-potluck-yet) with an email, Potluck uses that address for two things only:

- **Their invite email**, with the link to take their seat, and **one reminder** if someone asks for it. Each one carries an unsubscribe link.
- **Matching them up.** If they sign up and confirm that address, their seat becomes theirs on its own.

Nothing else is ever sent there, and nobody in the group sees the full address. Each person can send 20 invite emails a day at most (see [The daily invite limit](/groups/invites/#the-daily-invite-limit)).

The address is cleared when:

- **they take their seat.** The seat is gone, and their own account takes its place.
- **the seat is removed.** Its link stops working and nothing more goes to that address. If the group's history still names the seat, its name stays there.
- **the email is changed.** The new address replaces it, and the old link stops working.
- **the group is gone for good.** Its seats go with it. A group deleted from settings waits 30 days in case someone [restores it](/groups/settings/#restore-a-deleted-group) first.

## Blocks are never shown

Blocking someone is between you and Potluck. Potluck never tells anyone they were blocked. Wherever a block stops them, they see the same thing anyone would see for a closed link or a stranger: an invite that looks revoked, or **You can only split with friends.** Only you can see your list, on **Me** under [Blocked people](/account/#blocked-people).

## Two-step sign in protects everything

With [two-step sign in](/account/#two-step-sign-in) on, a password or sign-in link alone gets nobody into your account. Until the code from your authenticator app is in, Potluck shows and changes nothing: no groups, no expenses, no plans, no live updates, and no files, like receipts. Your email, password and two-step settings cannot be changed either.

This is checked by Potluck's servers, not just the app, so it holds even against someone who skips the app and talks to the servers directly.

## Security emails

Potluck emails you when:

- your password changes,
- your email changes,
- two-step sign in is turned on or off.

If a recovery code turns two-step sign in off, you also get a **Two-step sign in is off** notice in your inbox and on your devices, and by email once email notifications are on.

These always arrive. They have no setting on **Me** and no unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing from other emails does not stop them. If one surprises you, [change your password](/account/#change-your-password) right away.

## Things worth knowing

- **Profile photos** are stored at a link that is hard to guess but not secret. Anyone who has seen the link can open the photo.
- **Invite links** show the group's name, emoji, member count and first names to whoever opens them. Make links that run out (see [Inviting people](/groups/invites/#change-what-a-link-allows)) and revoke the ones you do not need.
- **Your calendar feed link** shows your plans' titles, times, places and group names to whoever has it. Keep it to yourself, and [reset it](/account/#reset-the-link) if it leaks.
- **Receipt photos** can contain card digits, addresses or location data. Crop before you upload.

## What leaves the app

- **Push notifications** pass through your browser maker's push service (Google, Mozilla or Apple). They are encrypted for your device, so that service cannot read them.
- **Exchange rates** are fetched from a public source of reference rates. Nothing about you is sent.
- **Emails** for sign-in links, password resets, email changes and [security alerts](#security-emails) are sent by Potluck's email provider. Once email notifications are on, so are the notification emails you get. Unlike push, they are not encrypted for your device: they carry the update itself, like an expense, payment, comment or your balances, and your mail service and the email provider can read them. To stop them, turn off the **Email** switches on **Me** (see [What to hear about](/notifications/#what-to-hear-about)).
- **Calendar files** you download, and Google Calendar links you open, contain the plan's details. That copy is yours to manage.
- **Backups and spreadsheets** you [export](/account/#export-your-data) are saved to your device. That copy is yours to manage too.

## Deleting your data

You can delete your account at any time from **Me**. Your profile, photo and email are removed, and most of your personal data with them. Shared money records stay so your friends' balances do not change, labeled **Former member**. The full list is on [Your account](/account/#what-happens-to-shared-tabs).

## Open source

Every line of Potluck's code is public on [GitHub](https://github.com/Fermulate/potluck) under the AGPL-3.0 license. Anyone can check how it handles data. To report a security problem, follow the project's [security policy](https://github.com/Fermulate/potluck/security/policy) instead of opening a public issue.
